Our Brand Story

From a shop in Yeola to your wardrobe

Very Much Indian was started in 2016 by Slony Gambhir, who grew up inside her father's textile shop in Yeola — the town at the centre of the Paithani revival. She left a corporate career, borrowed twenty-five sarees from her father, and sold every one. The house has bought the same way ever since: straight from the loom, one weaver at a time.

Today VMI works with over four thousand weaver families across Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka, Gujarat and Bihar — the same relationships that put the saree you're looking at on this page.

“

Every saree we sell is bought from the loom that made it. No middlemen, no mills, no shortcuts — only the weave, and the woman who carries it.

Slony Gambhir · Founder, Very Much Indian

A Shopkeeper's Daughter

How We Got Here

A fourteen-year-old sold her first saree through a car window in Yeola. Nine years later she built a company on the same rule: buy only from the weaver who made it.

The Woman Behind It

She sold her first saree at fourteen, through a car window

Long before Very Much Indian existed, a woman pulled over outside Slony Gambhir's father's shop in Yeola with a crying toddler in the back seat. Slony, fourteen and minding the counter, walked out to the car, spent fifteen minutes talking her through the drape, and sold her a ₹15,000 Paithani right there through the window. That same customer still walks into her father's store today.

It wasn't an accident. Slony grew up inside that shop — she could balance its accounts before she could manage a classroom. Textiles were in the family long before they were in her business plan.

What followed looked nothing like a saree shop: a BTech, an MBA, a decade in the corporate world. It paid well and fit her badly. In 2016 she quit, and asked her father for twenty-five sarees on credit to sell on her own. He refused — the salary was safer, he said. Her husband told her to go anyway.

She sold every single one. That first batch, out of her own house in Pune, became Very Much Indian — and the company still sources the exact same way she did on day one: no distributors, no middlemen, straight from the weaver's loom.

"Her father refused to hand over twenty-five sarees on credit. She took them anyway, sold them all, and never looked back."

Returns & Exchange

The same policy live on verymuchindian.com, in plain language.

Starting a return

Write to us at info@verymuchindian.com or on WhatsApp at 74100 27616 — returns raised over social media or by phone can't be processed. Once approved, you have 48 hours to hand the parcel to the courier, and it's worth keeping the courier receipt as your proof of handover. Refunds are issued 4–7 days after the piece reaches our Pune warehouse.

Exchanging a saree

Same contact — email or WhatsApp with your order number. We arrange the pickup, and the replacement ships within 5–7 working days. Moving to a higher-priced piece? We adjust the difference. Moving to a lower-priced one? The balance comes back as a credit note. Exchanged items can't be exchanged or returned a second time.

What isn't eligible

Sarees with fall or edging done, sale/discounted pieces, customised or personalised orders, and anything showing physical damage on your end. Since every piece is handloom, small hand-dye and weave irregularities versus the photo aren't treated as defects.

Higher-value pieces

Exclusive sarees and pieces ₹10,000 and above are only returnable for a genuine manufacturing defect. Sarees above ₹50,000 are final sale. Pre-ordered pieces are refundable only if they arrive damaged. Cancel before dispatch, and you get a full refund back.
